How it works

Alcohol evaporates from felt near the top. The cold, dark floor creates a supersaturated region just above it. Ionizing particles leave trails of condensed droplets, revealed by side lighting.

Thermal sizing

At an illustrative 8 V × 4 A per module, four modules draw 128 W total. Assuming 5–15 W total cold load and about 4 W pump heat, the loop must reject roughly 137–147 W. Holding the hot interfaces near 30 °C at 22 °C ambient requires approximately 0.055 K/W overall effective resistance, including block interfaces.

These are sizing estimates. A real TEC curve or loaded test must establish whether each module can pump its share of the load across about 58 K. A 240 mm radiator is a starting candidate, not a verified rating at this small temperature difference.

Water loop

Pump → block A → block B → radiator → reservoir. At 0.5 L/min, 145 W produces roughly a 4.2 °C coolant rise. A target of 1 L/min reduces that to about 2.1 °C. Free-flow pump specifications do not establish installed flow.

Release checks

Confirm all four module curves and matched heights, block coplanarity, clamping, actual coolant flow, radiator capacity, glass joints and gasket compatibility. Leak-test with TEC power off. Use aluminum-compatible inhibited coolant and compatible fittings throughout; the copper cold plate is outside the coolant loop. Verify the assembled active floor stays below −25 °C for 15 minutes with alcohol present.
